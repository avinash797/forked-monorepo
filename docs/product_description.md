# Forked — Product Description

## One-Liner

Forked ranks dishes, not restaurants. It tells you the best specific dish near you in under 30 seconds.

---

## The Problem

Every food app today rates restaurants. But nobody eats a restaurant — they eat a dish. You might love the gumbo at one place and hate their po'boy. Yelp, Google, and every other review platform buries that signal under 4.2-star averages, paragraphs of text, and photos of lattes.

The result: people stand on the sidewalk scrolling through contradictory reviews, still unsure what to order. Decision fatigue wins. They default to the safe choice or whatever's trending — not what's actually best.

Meanwhile, the hole-in-the-wall with the best fried chicken in the city stays hidden because they don't have a pretty Instagram or enough Google reviews.

## The Insight

The most useful food recommendation anyone can give is not "go to this restaurant." It's **"order the gumbo at Dooky Chase."** A specific dish at a specific place.

People already talk like this. They argue about it at dinner tables. "Best po'boy in the city? Parkway, no question." "You're crazy, Domilise's." These debates are real, passionate, and ongoing — but they've never had a platform.

Forked is that platform.

---

## What Forked Does

Forked lets people rate individual dishes — not restaurants — through a quick, game-like experience. No star ratings. No long reviews. Just a fast comparison: **"Which gumbo wins?"**

Users snap a photo, tell us how they felt about a dish (loved it, it was okay, didn't like it), and then compare it head-to-head against other dishes of the same type they've tried. A few taps, and they're done. Their personal ranking updates automatically.

All of those individual rankings feed into a community leaderboard: **the definitive list of the best gumbo, po'boy, fried chicken, muffuletta, and crawfish étouffée in New Orleans.**

### For the person deciding what to eat right now:
Open the app. See the #1 gumbo near you. See how confident the community is in that ranking. Go eat it.

### For the food obsessive:
Build your personal ranked list of every gumbo you've ever tried. See your #1 highlighted as your "Best Ever." Share it.

### For the tourist:
No account needed to view the leaderboard. Just see what's best nearby and go.

---

## How It Works (The User Experience)

1. **Eat** — Try a dish at a restaurant
2. **Snap** — Take a photo of the dish
3. **Compare** — Play a quick "This vs That" game against other dishes you've tried
4. **Rank** — Your score and the community leaderboard update automatically

The comparison step is where the magic happens. Instead of asking "rate this on a scale of 1 to 10" (which means something different to everyone), we show you two dishes side by side and ask: **"Which one wins?"** It's fast, it's fun, and it produces dramatically more honest data than any numeric rating ever could.

Most ratings take under 30 seconds. It feels like swiping through a game, not filling out a form.

---

## What Makes Forked Different

### Dishes, not restaurants
Every other food app rates the restaurant as a whole. Forked rates the actual thing you eat. This is a fundamentally different and more useful unit of information.

### No star ratings
Stars are broken. "What does 4 stars even mean?" Forked never asks users for a number. Scores are generated from real comparisons — which dish won when put head to head. The rankings reflect actual preference, not arbitrary scoring.

### Photo-required
Every rating includes a photo. This creates a beautiful, trustworthy visual record and naturally filters out fake or low-effort ratings. If you didn't take a photo, you probably weren't there.

### Scores that mean what they say
A dish scored 8.5 was genuinely loved by the people who rated it. A dish scored 3.0 was genuinely disliked. The system guarantees this — scores can't drift into meaningless territory no matter how many ratings exist.

### Neighborhood-level specificity
"Best gumbo in New Orleans" is useful. "Best gumbo in Tremé" is a reason to walk out the door right now.

## Launch Market: New Orleans

### Why NOLA

New Orleans is the perfect proving ground:

- **Iconic dishes with fierce local opinions.** People argue about gumbo the way other cities argue about sports teams.
- **Deep hole-in-the-wall culture.** The best food isn't always at the most visible places. Locals know this. Forked surfaces it.
- **Major tourist destination.** Millions of visitors every year asking one question: "Where should I eat?" The leaderboard answers that instantly.
- **Anti-Yelp sentiment.** NOLA food people don't trust star ratings. They trust other food people. Forked is built for them.
- **Compact geography.** Easy to seed meaningful data quickly.

### Launch Categories (5 Dishes)

| Dish | Why It Matters |
|------|---------------|
| Gumbo | The defining NOLA dish. Everyone has an opinion. |
| Po'boy | High frequency, legendary debates (Parkway vs. Domilise's). |
| Fried Chicken | Willie Mae's territory. Deeply emotional. |
| Muffuletta | Central Grocery vs. everyone else. |
| Crawfish Étouffée | Seasonal, passionate fanbase. |

Starting with just five categories is intentional. Forked goes deep before going wide. We want to be the undeniable authority on these five dishes before adding more.

## Target Users

### Primary: The Local Food Obsessive
They've tried gumbo at 15 restaurants. They have opinions. They want a way to track, rank, and prove those opinions. Forked gives them a personal leaderboard and a voice in the community rankings.

### Secondary: The Food-Motivated Tourist
3.6 million visitors come to New Orleans every year. Most of them will ask "where should I eat?" at least once a day. Forked gives them a confident, photo-backed answer without needing to create an account or read a single review.

### Tertiary: The Casual Diner
They don't obsess over food, but they eat out regularly and want to make better choices. The leaderboard is their cheat code.

## Go-to-Market Strategy

### Phase 1: Seeding (Pre-Launch)
Before the app goes public, a small group of local food enthusiasts ("Founding Forks") will seed the database with 200+ dish ratings across the five categories. This ensures that on day one, the leaderboard already has meaningful, trustworthy data. Nobody downloads a food app to an empty leaderboard.

### Phase 2: Closed Beta (Local-Only)
Distribution through the places locals actually go — bars, corner stores, dive spots. QR codes and stickers at hole-in-the-wall restaurants. Outreach to bartenders, servers, and kitchen staff — the people who actually know where to eat.

**The message:** *"This app ranks dishes, not restaurants. Help us settle arguments."*

### Phase 3: Public Launch
Publish "Forked's Top 10 Gumbo in New Orleans" on social media. Include one or two controversial picks intentionally. The call to action: **"Disagree? Download Forked and vote."**

Food arguments drive downloads. Controversy is the growth engine.

### Phase 4: Tourist Capture
SEO-optimized landing pages ("Best Gumbo in New Orleans 2026"). The leaderboard is publicly viewable without an account. Tourists discover the app through search, use it during their trip, and some convert to active users.

### Restaurant Outreach
Reach out to smaller, under-the-radar spots: "You're ranked #7 for best gumbo in New Orleans." They become organic promoters because the ranking is about food quality — not ambiance, service, or paid placement.

## The Vision

Forked starts with five dishes in one city. But the ambition is much larger.

**Phase 1 (v0.1):** Core experience. 5 dishes. New Orleans only.
**Phase 2 (v0.2):** Expand to 10 dish types. Add Houston and Austin.
**Phase 3 (v0.3):** Personalized recommendations powered by taste data. "You tend to love dark-roux, seafood-heavy gumbo — try this place."
**Phase 4 (v0.4):** Social layer. Follow local food experts, not influencers.
**Phase 5 (v1.0):** National rollout. Restaurant dashboards. API for press and media partnerships.

The long-term play: Forked becomes the **source of truth** for "what should I eat." Not restaurant ratings. Not influencer picks. Real rankings, built from real comparisons, by real people who actually ate the food and took the photo.

## Competitive Positioning

| | Yelp / Google | Beli | Forked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What it rates** | Restaurants | Restaurants | Individual dishes |
| **Rating method** | Stars + reviews | Comparisons | Comparisons |
| **Signal quality** | Noisy (ambiance, service, parking mixed in) | Better, but compares unlike things | Clean (gumbo vs. gumbo only) |
| **Verification** | None | None | Photo required |
| **Useful in 30 seconds** | Rarely | Sometimes | Always |
| **Hole-in-the-wall discovery** | Poor (algorithm favors volume) | Moderate | Strong (quality wins, not review count) |

### Why not Yelp?
Yelp rates restaurants, not food. A 4-star restaurant can have mediocre gumbo. A 3-star restaurant can have the best po'boy in the city. Yelp can't tell you that.

### Why not Beli?
Beli improved on Yelp with comparisons, but it still compares restaurants. A fine-dining French bistro competing against a crawfish shack makes no sense. Forked only compares like with like — the same dish type, head to head.

## Brand Identity

### Tone
Confident. Opinionated. Local. Anti-corporate. Forked sounds like a friend who knows where to eat, not a tech company trying to disrupt dining.

### Core Messaging Pillars

1. **"Rank dishes, not restaurants."** — The founding distinction.
2. **"Settle the argument."** — Every city has food debates. Forked is where they get resolved.
3. **"The real list."** — Not sponsored. Not paid. Ranked by people who ate it and proved it with a photo.
4. **"Hole-in-the-wall energy."** — Forked celebrates the places that don't need Instagram to be great.

### Visual Identity
Photo-forward. The dish is always the hero. Community photos — not professional food photography — are the visual language. This is real food from real people.

## The Moat

Forked's defensibility is not a feature. It's the data.

Every comparison a user makes generates a signal that no other platform has: dish-level preference data, verified with photos, anchored to real sentiment. As that data compounds, the leaderboard becomes more accurate, more trusted, and harder to replicate.

The more people use Forked, the better the rankings get. The better the rankings get, the more people trust it. The more people trust it, the more they use it.

That flywheel — once spinning — is the moat.
